So this is the first journal entry of when Hurintha and I were split.

​It wasn't just "mould." It was a bioluminescent, subterranean lichen growth following a strict, unnatural geometric fracture in the basalt. To an untrained eye, it looked like grime; to a surveyor holding the fading blueprint of the world's root system, it was a living topographical marker. The tectonic strata in this side passage were far too clean, swept smooth by something deliberate rather than carved by natural subterranean drainage.

While Hurintha vanished down the corridor driven by monastic spite and childhood curiosity, I paused to log the anomalous spore drift. Theon and our escort—the so-called "princess"—were keeping pace just behind, warily watching the bioluminescent gloom while the rest of the vanguard was busy tangling with whatever crawling horrors were lurking back at the main fork.

The silence didn't last. A resonant, echoing crash rattled the cavern walls ahead—the unmistakable acoustic signature of Hurintha introducing her shoulder to a fortified door.

Rounding the bend with Theon and the princess in tow, the air shifted from damp stone to aged vellum, ozone, and decades of disturbed parchment dust. We found Hurintha standing in the threshold of a cluttered subterranean redoubt, accompanied by an eccentric, frantic wizard named Euzarg who was already pacing the floor and cursing an archivist named Trevandor. The room was choked with haphazardly stacked crates and ancient tomes blocking the sanctuary's worktables.

Hurintha had her eye on an arcane amulet, but the moment my boots crossed the threshold, the Codex at my hip resonated with a low, familiar frequency. Dusty tomes, abandoned libraries, and eccentric keepers of forgotten knowledge are precisely the kind of clutter that draws my attention.

The Journal of Valerian: Subterranean Strata and the Living Line

Three cycles of waking and sleeping in absolute dark. Or perhaps three weeks. Standard surface reckoning means nothing when you are calibrating against tectonic resonance instead of sunlight.

While Hurintha, Theon, and the rest were occupied with the mad wizard Euzarg and his hoarded sanctuary, the Codex of the First Root ceased merely humming. It bit.

The heat radiating from its bark-bound plates reached a fever pitch, burning straight through my leather harness until the runic grain of the cover scorched into the skin beneath my ribs. It wasn't a distress signal; it was a directional vector. A hairline fracture behind Euzarg's deepest book-alcove was bleeding cold air and high-frequency static—an active planar data leak cutting right through the under-vault's basalt foundation.
​I left them to their crates and broke off into the fissures alone.

Down here, the world does not follow stonecutter logic. The ley lines of the Upper Underdark are fractured, buckled under the weight of millennia of planar compression. Following them meant climbing down chimney-narrow faults where a single misstep would drop you into black chasms with no bottom, mapping anomalies where gravity pulled sideways against mineral-heavy cavern walls. Every hundred paces, the Codex demanded tethering.

​I drove wooden pins into the living rock, anchoring the baseline survey. Each time I opened the text, the parchment bled that faint, amber-gold pulse, transcribing the subterranean topography directly onto the pages. But the flow wasn't one-way. With every stratum mapped, the First Root pushed back into my marrow. My senses stretched across miles of porous stone, picking up the distant, wet scuttle of subterranean predators, the rhythmic drip of calcifying caverns, and the distinct, unnatural hum of the Order's planar scanners sweeping the deep dark above.
The bond settled somewhere in the subterranean silent hours. The book is no longer just a stolen artifact slung at my belt; it is an internal compass, locked directly into the subterranean fault lines. My palms still carry the phantom vibration of the granite, and my eyes have adjusted to the cold spectrum of bioluminescent drift.

I've mapped the route back. The survey pins hold, the telemetry is logged, and the Codex has gone silent again—heavy, watchful, and satisfied. Now it's time to re-emerge, find where the party's trail picked up, and see what fresh mess they've managed to stumble into while my back was turned.
