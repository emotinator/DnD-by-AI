### **Read Aloud: Entering the Sanctuary**

The air inside is cold and unnaturally still.

Rows of broken stone benches lead toward a vast circular chamber where the ceiling has fallen open to the sky. Rainwater pools across the floor, reflecting pale daylight in trembling patches. Once, this place must have been magnificent — a hall of law, oaths, and judgment. Now it is a mausoleum of forgotten truths.

Along the walls stand statues of armored judges and sworn knights. Most have been damaged, their faces chipped away or eroded smooth. One statue still grips a stone tablet against its chest, but the writing upon it has been scraped clean.

Behind the main altar, a great mural survives in fragments. Figures kneel before a blazing lantern while scribes record their words upon endless scrolls. The lantern itself has been deliberately defaced.

Dust lies thick across everything.

Except for a narrow trail of disturbed footprints leading toward a collapsed archway behind the altar.

### **📖 Read Aloud: The Hall of Records**

Beyond the altar, a staircase descends into darkness.

The lower chamber is not a crypt.

It is an archive.

Stone shelves line the walls from floor to ceiling, though most scrolls have long since rotted to dust. Broken tablets litter the floor beside collapsed reading tables. Here and there, bronze nameplates remain bolted to empty shelves: Oaths. Judgments. Genealogies. Treaties.

At the center of the chamber stands a low stone pedestal.

Upon it rests a bronze lantern.

Cold. Dark. Untouched by rust.

Around the pedestal, carved into the floor in a circle, are faint words in ancient Chondathan:

“Justice remembers.”

### **The First Forge**

The forge is unlike anything the party has seen.

The walls are smooth black stone, fused as though they were once molten. There are no tool racks, no discarded ingots, no soot. Everything has been meticulously preserved—not by caretakers, but by time itself.

At the center stands an anvil.

It is immense. Ancient. Untouched.

No rust.

No dust.

Only the faint impression that countless master smiths once stood before it.

The forge is utterly cold.

No embers.

No ash.

No heat.

No sound.

Even footsteps seem strangely muted here.

---

As the party explores, nothing happens.

Until someone carrying the Flame wanders within several feet of the anvil.

The flame does not grow brighter.

Instead...

the forge awakens.

Not with roaring fire.

With memory.

Tiny veins of orange light spread through cracks in the stone floor.

The bellows inhale by themselves.

The hammer hanging above the anvil gives a single, deliberate swing.

*CLANG.*

The sound echoes impossibly long.

For just a heartbeat...

the forge burns exactly as it did thousands of years ago.

The room is filled with warm golden light.

The players might even glimpse faint silhouettes—not ghosts, but impressions. Dwarven smiths at work, apprentices passing tools, priests chanting over newly forged relics. They never notice the party. The vision lasts only an instant, like a memory replaying itself.

Then the fire dies.

Silence returns.

---

On the face of the anvil, letters glow.

Not carved.

Revealed.

As though the metal itself remembers.

> **One Keeper returns.**

The words remain only a few seconds before fading back into black iron.

Nothing else happens.

---

### **Why this is so powerful**

Notice what the message *doesn't* say.

It doesn't say:

* "You are the chosen one."  
* "Save the world."  
* "Seek the prison."

Instead, it quietly confirms one astonishing fact:

**The Flame has been here before.**

The forge recognizes it.

Not the bearer.

The Flame.

That means someone carried this sacred fire here in an age before recorded history. Suddenly the little flame they've been nurturing isn't just a magical torch—it's part of an ancient stewardship stretching back to the first days of civilization.

---

### **One final touch**

As the forge fades, ask each player privately:

> "What's the oldest memory your character has?"

Let them answer.

Then say:

> "For a moment... you remember something older."

Don't explain it. Let them describe whatever flashes into their mind—a mountain being hollowed out for the first time, Moradin striking the First Anvil, a procession of Keepers carrying living flame through halls of fresh-cut stone.

Then it's gone.

None of them can quite recall what they saw.

Just the feeling that it mattered.
